ChargeGuard

ChargeGuard holds your MacBook battery at 80% at Home and at the Office. At other places, it charges to 100%.

It finds the place from your Wi-Fi router. A widget on the desktop shows the battery and has all settings.

NOTE: ChargeGuard is a beta. It is tested on one MacBook Pro M5 with macOS 27.2.1.

1Why ChargeGuard

A lithium-ion battery wears out faster when it stays at 100% for a long time, and when it is hot. A MacBook that is on the charger all day at a desk stays full and warm for hours. A limit near 80% makes the battery last more years.

But one fixed limit does not fit a normal day.

  • At home and at the office, the Mac is on the charger for hours. It needs a limit, for example 80%.
  • At the end of the office day, you need a full battery for the trip home and the evening. You must remember to remove the limit before you leave, and to set it again the next day.
  • At a café, on a trip, or at a client, you need 100%, because the next charger is far away.

macOS has a native charge limit, but it is one fixed value. It does not know where you are, and it does not change during the day. Optimized Battery Charging learns your habits, but you cannot control when it charges to 100%.

ChargeGuard does this work automatically.

  1. It finds the place from your Wi-Fi router. It does not use GPS or Location Services.
  2. It holds the limit at Home and at the Office, and it charges to 100% at other places.
  3. On work days, it charges to 100% before the office day ends. The next day, it goes back to the limit.
  4. It shows what it does on a desktop widget, so you always know why the Mac charges or does not charge.

It is free and open source, so you can read what it does to your Mac.

2How the policy decides

The policy is the function decide in src/policy.ts. It has no side effects. It takes the battery data, the config, the saved state, and the time. It returns the target, the mode, the native limit, the LED color, and the notices. The daemon applies the result.

Places

The place comes from the MAC address of the default gateway, which is your router. ChargeGuard does not use GPS. The function resolvePlace works in this order.

Place rules, in the order that the code checks them
ConditionPlace
The place mode is not autoThe place that you chose: home, office, or away. This also holds with no network.
There is no router addressoffline
The router address is not a valid MACaway
The router is saved for Homehome
The router is saved for Officeoffice
Any other routeraway

The base target is the limit of the place, or the general limit. The default is 80%. At away and offline, the base target is awayLimit. The default is 100%.

Decision order

Each step can change the target. A later step wins over an earlier step. The list follows the code.

  1. Place and base target. Find the place. Set the target to the base target.

  2. Plug and unplug events. Compare the charger state with the last tick. A plug-in sends a notice that tells the place and the target. An unplug sends a notice with the battery percent. An unplug also cancels a top-up that you started by hand.

  3. Office top-up. The place must be office. The target is 100% from the end of the work day minus the lead time, until the end plus the grace time. Defaults: days Monday to Friday, start 08:00, end 17:00, lead 90 min, grace 120 min.

  4. Calendar trip top-up. If calendar trips are on and a trip is near, the target is 100% from the top-up start until 30 min after the leave time. See section 3.

  5. Schedule. A rule fires in a 10 min window that starts at the time of the rule. A rule does not start while an override from the user is active. A rule makes one of four overrides: top-up, limit, discharge, or calibration.

  6. Overrides. An override replaces the target. See the table below.

  7. Heat. The battery is hot at 35 °C or more. Charging stops. It starts again at 32 °C or less. Heat protection does not apply during pause, discharge, and the discharge steps of calibration.

  8. Native limit. The native limit is the target, as a whole number from 10 to 100. During heat protection, it is the current percent.

  9. Safety. Below 10%, the adapter is always on. The policy never turns the adapter off below 10%.

  10. Mode, LED, Low Power Mode, sleep, notices. The policy sets the mode from the table in the next part. Then it sets the LED color, Low Power Mode, sleep prevention, and the notices that you did not turn off.

Source: src/policy.ts, function decide, steps 1 to 15 in the code comments. src/config.ts, DEFAULT_CONFIG.

Overrides

The four override kinds, and pause
OverrideWhat the policy doesHow it starts
Pause Target 100%, native limit 100%, adapter on. Mode paused. ChargeGuard stops management and macOS charges normally. Heat protection is off. cg pause. cg resume ends it.
Top up Charges to 100% one time. A schedule rule limit:N uses the same override with the target N. It ends when you disconnect the charger. cg topup. cg topup stop ends it.
Discharge Uses the battery while the charger is connected, down to N%. The adapter is off. If the lid is closed, the discharge waits until the lid opens, unless dischargeInClamshell is on. The default is off. The override ends at N%. cg discharge N. N is 10 to 99.
Calibration Four steps. 1: discharge to 15%. 2: charge to 100%. 3: hold at 100% for 60 min. 4: discharge to the limit. The charger must stay connected. cg calibrate. cg calibrate stop ends it.

3Calendar trips

ChargeGuard can charge to 100% before you leave for a meeting at another place. This feature is off by default. It is new in version 0.2.0.

It reads meetings from the macOS calendar through EventKit. A meeting must have a time and a physical location. The script scripts/cg-calendar.js skips all-day events, events with no location, and online meetings. It also skips a location that Apple Maps cannot find. A room name such as "Meeting Room 3" is an example.

Two estimates

  1. Drive time. Apple Maps gives the drive time from your saved Home or Office address to the meeting place. The script asks only when the current place is Home or Office and it has an address.
  2. Charge time. ChargeGuard estimates how long the battery needs to reach 100%. It uses the live battery power while the Mac charges. Then it uses the charge power that it learned. If neither exists, it uses 30% of the charger power.

Formulas

leaveAt    = start - travelMin - arriveBufferMin
topupStart = leaveAt - ceil(minutesToFull × 1.2)

The factor 1.2 adds 20% for a margin, because charging is slower above 80%. The policy applies it to the time that is not yet rounded, so it does not round twice. The names are from windowFor in src/policy.ts. The defaults are below.

Calendar settings
SettingDefaultRangeMeaning
calendar.arriveBufferMin100 to 120Minutes to arrive before the meeting starts.
calendar.fallbackTravelMin450 to 300Travel time when no estimate exists.
calendar.graceMin300 to 240The top-up stays on until the leave time plus this many minutes.
calendar.lookaheadH241 to 72Only meetings that start inside this many hours count.
calendar.minTravelMin100 to 120A known drive time below this value is too near. No top-up starts.

More rules from the code

  • If the drive time is known and is below 10 min, ChargeGuard does not top up.
  • If the drive time is unknown, ChargeGuard uses 45 min. This happens at an Away place, or when you saved no address.
  • If the battery cannot reach 100% before the leave time, the status shows that the top-up is too late.
  • The script keeps a travel time for 60 min before it asks Apple Maps again.

Turn it on

In the widget:

  1. Click the gear on the widget. Open Settings and then Calendar.
  2. Type your Home and Office address.
  3. Click Grant calendar access. Click Allow when macOS asks.
  4. Turn Calendar trips on.

On the command line:

~/.chargeguard/bin/cg set places.home.address "1 Example Street, City"
~/.chargeguard/bin/cg set places.office.address "2 Example Avenue, City"
~/.chargeguard/bin/cg calendar on
~/.chargeguard/bin/cg trips

macOS asks one time to give Calendar access to osascript. The widget runs the calendar script every 10 minutes. The script writes the trips to ~/.chargeguard/trips.json. The daemon reads that file.

If it does not work

Access denied
Open System Settings, Privacy & Security, Calendars. Allow osascript or the app that runs the widget.
No trip shows
Run cg calendar refresh. It shows the events that ChargeGuard found and the events that it skipped.
A meeting does not show
Make sure that the event has a time and a real street address. Room names are skipped.
The drive time is 45 minutes
Save an address for the place where you are, or go to Home or Office.

4Privacy

  • ChargeGuard does not use GPS or Location Services.
  • Your addresses stay on your Mac. Only Apple Maps gets them. Apple Maps does the address search and the drive time.
  • Your meeting places go only to Apple Maps. Nothing else leaves your Mac.
  • The place comes from the MAC address of your router. The code reads it with route and arp. No Wi-Fi permission is necessary.
  • ChargeGuard reads the router only at start, on plug-in, on wake, on a network change, or after 10 minutes on charger power.
  • ChargeGuard never reads the router on battery power.
  • The widget uses local React and Babel files. It needs no network.
  • The source code is open. You can read what ChargeGuard does to your Mac.

Source: README.md, sections Calendar trips and How it works. src/net.ts. widget/standalone/vendor.

8The root helper

The script scripts/cg-helper is the only part that runs as root. The installer puts it at /usr/local/libexec/chargeguard/cg-helper. The daemon calls it with sudo -n. The helper must run as root and must get exactly two arguments. It validates every argument. It runs nothing else.

Commands that cg-helper accepts
CommandAllowed valuesAction
limit An integer from 10 to 100 Writes MCLFeatureState 1 and mclLimitValue N in the domain com.apple.smartcharging.topoffprotection. Then it sends the notice com.apple.smartcharging.defaultschanged.
adapter on or off Writes the SMC key CHIE: 00 for on, 08 for off.
led system, off, green, or orange Writes the SMC key ACLC: 00, 01, 03, or 04.
lowpower 0 or 1 Runs pmset -a lowpowermode with the value.

Limit 100 lifts the macOS limit

The command limit 100 sets MCLFeatureState to 1 and mclLimitValue to 100. A limit of 100% is not a real limit. The script comment says that MCLFeatureState 0 did not lift the limit. This fix is in version 0.2.0. A top-up to 100% and the uninstaller choice Charge to 100% use it.

Safeguards in the installer

  • Sudoers rule. The file is /etc/sudoers.d/chargeguard. Mode 440, owner root. It holds one line: <user> ALL=(root) NOPASSWD: /usr/local/libexec/chargeguard/cg-helper. The rule covers only this helper. visudo -cf checks the rule before the installer puts it in place.
  • SHA-256 of smc. scripts/install-root.sh compares the smc binary with this hash: e3b4392c966dee700f3e1d6cc3812c0d487954c3d6d77b91f521a9590aadae44. It stops if the hash does not match.
  • /usr/local check. If /usr/local or /usr/local/libexec exists, the owner must be root. Group and others must not be able to write to it. If they can, the script stops. If a user process can write to the folder, it can replace cg-helper and get root.
  • Quarantine flag. The script removes the download flag from smc and cg-helper. Then it runs smc one time to test it.

Source: scripts/cg-helper. scripts/install-root.sh, steps 2 to 6. Release notes for v0.2.0 and v0.1.0.

9Install

Requirements

  • A Mac with Apple silicon, M1 or later.
  • A macOS version with the native charge limit. The command is pmset -g battlimit. The installer checks this.
  • An admin account. The installer asks for the password one time.

Steps

  1. Download ChargeGuard-0.2.0.dmg from the release page.
  2. Open the disk image. Double-click Install ChargeGuard.
  3. If macOS says that it cannot check the app, click Done. Open System Settings and then Privacy & Security. Scroll down and click Open Anyway. The app is not signed with an Apple developer account. That is why macOS asks.
  4. Click Install, and type your Mac password.
  5. For calendar trips, allow Calendar access when macOS asks.

The installer copies ChargeGuard to ~/.chargeguard. It starts two launchd agents: the daemon and the widget. You do not need Terminal, Homebrew, or Bun. The disk image includes them.

If a step fails

The installer shows the step, a fix, and a Show Log button. The log is ~/.chargeguard/install.log.

"smc Not Opened" or "Apple could not verify smc"
Click Done, and open Install ChargeGuard again. Version 0.1.1 and later remove the download flag from smc, so macOS does not block it. Open the new installer to repair an older install.
"Install ChargeGuard Not Opened"
Do install step 3 again.
You did not give the password
Open Install ChargeGuard again.

First setup

  1. Find the widget at the bottom left of the screen.
  2. Click the gear on the widget. The Settings panel opens.
  3. At home, click Use current Wi-Fi next to Home.
  4. At the office, click Use current Wi-Fi next to Office.

On a network that you did not save, the place is Away, and the Mac charges to 100%.

Update from version 0.1.x

Open Install ChargeGuard from the new disk image. This step is necessary, because the fix is in the root helper. Your settings stay.

Optional parts

  • Menu bar icon: install SwiftBar, then install ChargeGuard again.
  • Stop charging while the Mac sleeps: install sleepwatcher with Homebrew, then install ChargeGuard again.

Source: README.md, sections Requirements, Install, and First setup. packaging/README.txt. packaging/installer.applescript. Release notes for v0.1.1 and v0.2.0.

10Uninstall

Double-click Uninstall ChargeGuard in the disk image. Do install step 3 again if macOS asks. The dialog has two choices for the charge limit that macOS keeps.

Uninstall choices
ChoiceResult
Keep the limitmacOS keeps the charge limit that is set now.
Charge to 100%The uninstaller runs cg-helper limit 100 before it removes the helper. This lifts the macOS limit. It works from version 0.2.0.

The uninstaller does these jobs.

  • It turns the adapter on and sets the LED to the system mode.
  • It stops the daemon and the widget agents, and removes their launchd files.
  • It removes the Übersicht widget link and the SwiftBar plugin link.
  • It removes the cg lines from ~/.sleep and ~/.wakeup.
  • It removes ~/.chargeguard, which holds your config, state, and logs.
  • With administrator rights, it removes /usr/local/libexec/chargeguard and /etc/sudoers.d/chargeguard.

Source: packaging/uninstaller.applescript. scripts/uninstall.sh. README.md, section Uninstall.

12Releases

All releases are on the GitHub releases page. All three are beta releases.

0.2.0, 2026-11-14

New

  • Calendar trips. ChargeGuard reads your calendar meetings that have a physical location. Apple Maps gives the drive time from your Home or Office address. ChargeGuard then charges to 100% before you must leave.
  • The feature is off by default. Online meetings, all-day events, and events with no location are skipped.
  • cg trips shows the next trips and the computed times.
  • The charge-time estimate learns the real charge rate of your Mac.

Fixed

  • A top-up to 100% now lifts the macOS charge limit. Before, macOS kept the old limit, so office top-ups did not charge past it.
  • The Charge to 100% choice of the uninstaller now works for the same reason.

Update from 0.1.x: open Install ChargeGuard from the new disk image.

0.1.1, 2026-11-11

Fixed

  • macOS no longer blocks the smc tool with "smc Not Opened" or "Apple could not verify smc". The installer removes the download flag from smc and tests it.
  • If a step fails, the installer shows the step, a fix, and a Show Log button. The log is ~/.chargeguard/install.log.
  • The installer tests the battery helper before it reports success.
  • The widget shows plain messages that tell you to open the installer again.

0.1.0, 2026-11-11

  • First release. ChargeGuard holds the battery at 80% at Home and at the Office, and charges to 100% at other places.
  • It needs a Mac with Apple silicon, a macOS version with the native charge limit, and an admin account.
  • The installer puts a small root helper in /usr/local/libexec/chargeguard. It also adds a sudoers rule for that helper only. The uninstaller removes both.
  • The disk image includes Bun, MIT license, and the smc tool from the battery project, GNU GPL.
